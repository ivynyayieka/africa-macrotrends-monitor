BENIN FOCUSES ON THE INVESTMENT IN CULTURAL HERITAGE

Tourism Review Media · 1 Nov 2026

Over the past decade Benin has strategically invested heavily in its cultural heritage and tourism sector. This has been done with the aim of boosting economic growth and building soft power through its increased intangible cultural heritage. Specific legal changes have been made alongside attracting high-end hospitality projects to allow Benin to become a unique leader in the region.

West African countries have been highly vulnerable to external factors such as climate change and fluctuating global commodity prices. Benin has seen impressive growth over the years with the economy growing at over 6.5%. However, the country still suffers from volatility in cotton prices and Nigeria’s economic stability. This has resulted in frequent disruptions in the Beninese economy due to naira depreciation and trade restrictions from Nigeria.

To improve the balance of payments, the government has invested in the tourism and hotel industry. The sector development plan aims to increase tourism to contribute to 13.4% of GDP by 2030 from 6%. This has come with a total investment of €1.2 billion. The focus of Benin’s investment has been placed on its unique history and cultural heritage.

What 100 Economies Have Taught Us About Climate and Development

World Bank Blogs · 28 Oct 2026

The current El Niño is a reminder of how quickly climate shocks can become development challenges. Drought can wither crops and drain the reservoirs that power homes and factories. Heavy rainfall can wash away schools, roads, and other infrastructure. The effects vary from place to place, but the lesson is widely shared: countries need to build resilience through smart development that protects livelihoods, creates jobs, and drives growth.

That is the thinking behind the World Bank Group’s Country Climate and Development Reports (CCDRs). Driven by national development priorities, the series now covers 100 economies, with São Tomé and Príncipe the latest addition . Each CCDR supports countries with a practical roadmap for resilient, low-carbon growth that advances their wider development priorities, from jobs and energy security to better health. They are complemented by annual summary reports that highlight the key global insights, with four reports published so far on infrastructure and the macroeconomy , the interplay with nature and role of forestry, the vulnerability and agency of people , and the implications for jobs .

Reaching 100 economies is a milestone and a good moment to ask what we have learned. I see four lessons and two priorities for the road ahead.
