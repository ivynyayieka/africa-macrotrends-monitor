Rebuilding after the floods: How support and poultry farming transformed a family’s future

Save the Children · 13 Nov 2026

Jemila*, a 45-year-old mother of four living in the Afar Region of Ethiopia, saw her family’s stability shattered when catastrophic flooding in the Awash Basin swept away her property and livelihood. Left without the assets she depended on to support her children and grandson, she was selected to participate in the EU-funded "Sustainable Support to Persons Displaced by Conflict and Natural Disasters and Their Host Communities in the Afar Region of Ethiopia" project.

Through the project, Jemila* received improved-breed chickens and intensive training in modern poultry management practices. As a result, she has established a home-based poultry that provides both nutritious food and a reliable source of income. Today, the income generated from egg production helps cover her family’s essential healthcare and education expenses while strengthening their resilience to future climate-related shocks.

“The flooding that happened made us lose everything... it was very difficult for our whole family. Now, the support makes us work and earn to support my family.” Jemila*

Ethiopia: highlights from the October 2026 Dutchiopia Agri Newsletter

Agroberichten Buitenland · 9 Nov 2026

The Netherlands Pavilion was one of the highlights of the African Livestock Exhibition and Congress (ALEC), held in Addis Ababa from 29–31 October 2026. Organized by the Agricultural team of the Embassy of the Kingdom of the Netherlands, the pavilion brought together Dutch companies and organizations active in the livestock sector to showcase their expertise, innovative technologies, products and equipment for the dairy and poultry industries. It also provided an opportunity to explore business opportunities, establish new partnerships and facilitate knowledge exchange between Dutch and Ethiopian stakeholders.

ALEC is an important platform for Ethiopia, where livestock plays an important role in rural livelihoods, food security and the national economy. With growing demand for meat and milk, the sector offers opportunities for job creation, business development and investment. By bringing together producers, policymakers, businesses, research institutions and development partners, the event provided an opportunity to address sector challenges and explore solutions to strengthen livestock value chains. Through their participation, Dutch companies demonstrated how their expertise and technologies can contribute to this development, particularly through improved access to quality animal feed and inputs, modern dairy processing equipment, better production practices and stronger value chain linkages.

The Netherlands Pavilion was organized in close collaboration with SNV, the Netherlands Development Organization, and its Netherlands-supported BRIDGE+ program, which works to strengthen Ethiopia’s dairy value chain by supporting smallholder farmers and cooperatives to improve productivity, produce safe and high-quality milk. The collaboration highlighted the value of connecting Dutch private-sector expertise with local businesses and development initiatives to translate innovation and knowledge into practical solutions. It also reaffirmed the Netherlands’ commitment to supporting a more productive, inclusive and sustainable livestock sector in Ethiopia through collaboration and long-term partnerships.

Drought reshapes livelihoods of Kenya’s Daasanach pastoralists, study finds

news - Mongabay · 10 Nov 2026

The Daasanach people in the Turkana Basin of northern Kenya experienced the worst drought in 40 years between November 2020 and March 2023. The Daasanach are pastoralists who have lived semi-nomadically for roughly 6,000 years, supported by herds of livestock including cows, camels, donkeys, and goats.

A group of researchers worked with the Daasanach to study the effects of the drought on their culture and access to food. They examined water and food access as well as coping and dietary strategies for 515 adults and 384 children in communities around Lake Turkana in Kenya.

During the drought, livestock herds dropped precipitously, with little recovery by 2024. Prior to the drought in 2019, each household had an average of 24 sheep and goats; by 2022 and 2023, they had just eight on average. Similarly, before the drought, the average family had six cows, and during the drought, just two. Such livestock are central to the economy and food security of the Daasanach pastoralists. With fewer animals to feed themselves, many Daasanach families moved to the town of Illeret during the drought to be closer to food aid.

Making research count in Africa’s post-2030 agenda

Stockholm Environment Institute · 16 Nov 2026

Climate change and other development challenges require more scientific knowledge, a point firmly made at the 2026 Science Summit, a portion of which was held in Nairobi, co-hosted by the Stockholm Environment Institute. The implications are particularly stark for African nations, where investments in scientific knowledge that can help address these challenges are worryingly low.

Governments face mounting pressure to address climate change, food insecurity, energy access, and biodiversity loss, often with limited financial resources and many competing agendas. And yet the research needed to guide these decisions is rarely funded adequately or translated into policies and investments that deliver results.

As the UN begins developing its sustainable development agenda beyond 2030, Africa has an opportunity to address this disconnect. Research should not be an afterthought but instead should be seen as a vital investment in the continent’s development.
