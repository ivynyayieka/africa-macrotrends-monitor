How Bank of Ghana's monetary framework is slowing inflation

CNBC Africa · 21 May 2026

The Bank of Ghana’s 2025 financial results are reigniting debate over the cost of monetary stabilization, after the central bank disclosed a sterilization bill of roughly 16.7 billion cedis tied to efforts to absorb excess liquidity from the financial system.

But according to financial markets expert Norman Adu Bamfo, that large bill should not be viewed only as a red flag. Instead, he argues, it reflects a monetary framework that has been effective in bringing inflation down, supporting the cedi and restoring broader macroeconomic stability — even if the financial burden of maintaining that framework is becoming increasingly structural.

Speaking in a television interview, Bamfo said the Bank of Ghana’s latest results must be read alongside the macroeconomic progress Ghana has recorded over the past year. He pointed to a decline in inflation from 23.8% in December 2024 to 13.4% by December 2025, a 14.7% appreciation of the cedi against the U.S. dollar, and a sharp increase in gross international reserves from $9.11 billion, equivalent to four months of import cover, to $13.83 billion, or 5.7 months of import cover.

Ofodile: Africa must rethink how it defines jobs in harnessing youth population

CNBC Africa · 22 May 2026

Africa needs to rethink what it considers a “good job” if it is to unlock the full economic potential of its rapidly growing youth population, according to MTN Benin CEO Uche Ofodile, who argued that future employment growth will increasingly come from sectors often overlooked in traditional policy and corporate thinking.

Speaking to CNBC Africa on the sidelines of the Africa Soft Power Summit in Nairobi, Ofodile said the continent’s employment challenge cannot be solved by focusing narrowly on conventional white-collar corporate roles. Instead, she called for a broader definition of work that recognizes the economic value of industries such as beauty, fashion, entertainment and other creative sectors, while also preparing young Africans for shifts driven by technology.

Her comments come at a time when Africa is widely seen as one of the world’s most important future sources of labor, entrepreneurship and consumption, with demographic projections pointing to a youthful population that could become a major competitive advantage if effectively trained and absorbed into productive sectors.

flow – Deutsche Bank · 21 May 2026

When Guinea Conakry’s (Republic of Guinea’s) leader General Mamady Doumbouya obtained 87.6% of the vote in the presidential election on 28 December 2025, this delivered a seven-year mandate; 1 presenting an opportunity for the country to invest in itself and move more of its population out of poverty.

Despite its abundant natural resources – including diamonds, iron ore, gold and bauxite – a growing population (now exceeding 15 million), and optimum geographic location, more than half the Guinean population lives below the international poverty level of US$3.65 a day.

Situated in West Africa bordering six countries (Guinea-Bissau, Senegal, Mali, Sierra Leone, Liberia and Côte d’Ivoire), this sub-Saharan African republic is ideally situated for agriculture and trade – and is one of the richest countries in the world for mineral wealth. Not enough of this wealth, however, is invested in the country itself – a deficiency the new government has tasked itself with rectifying. Part of its policy is improving Guinea’s investment climate.
