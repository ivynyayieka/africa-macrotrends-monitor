Trump Has Deported Thousands in Third Country Removals

American Civil Liberties Union · 15 Sep 2026

The Trump administration has deported over 23,000 people over the last year and a half to countries they have no relationship with, where they may not speak the language, and where they generally have no legal status. Sometimes, it deports people to conflict zones or countries where they are likely to face persecution or even torture.

The Trump administration is spending tens of millions of dollars to fly people, shackled at times for over 50 hours , to “third countries” where they have no ties — even when they have won protection in U.S. immigration courts. The policies and agreements behind third country deportations are being negotiated behind closed doors, and some have never been made public.

On the one hand, what the U.S. government is really paying for is propaganda: The goal is to terrify immigrants into abandoning their cases and “self-deporting.” But at the same time, this administration is keeping its agreements, flight plans, and any information on who it has deported where and why as closely-held secrets. The secrecy is a strategy, and as we have seen with other regimes that participate in enforced disappearances, it is designed to hide these flights from court and public intervention, torment those on the flights, and terrify others waiting to find out if they will be next.

Orange links up West Africa

Telecoms · 30 Nov 2023

At AfricaTech, Orange formally launched its pan-West African network, designed to remove the need for complex cross-border interconnections in the region.

The Djoliba network is run and maintained from Dakar in Senegal, and connects a further seven countries: Burkina Faso, Côte d’Ivoire, Ghana, Guinea, Liberia, Mali and Nigeria. The infrastructure is natively interconnected with the domestic networks within those eight countries, which, according to Orange, is something of a game-changer for the region.

The lack of a cross-border network in West Africa meant that in order to provide a service between cities in separate countries, operators had to bring together services from various different providers using infrastructure that interconnected at a national border. A pan-regional network significantly simplifies that process.

Report links fishmeal production to decline in key species for Guinea-Bissau communities

news - Mongabay · 15 Sep 2026

Populations of sardinella, an important protein-rich fish, are in rapid decline in the waters off Guinea-Bissau in West Africa. The decline appears to be related to industrial fishing for two species of sardinella used to make fishmeal, according to a report by investigative news organization OCCRP.

In Guinea-Bissau, fish like the Madeiran sardinella ( Sardinella maderensis ) and round sardinella ( Sardinella aurita ) are among the primary ingredients in fishmeal, a processed fish product added to livestock feed for added protein and nutrients.

But sardinella is also a crucial food for people in Guinea-Bissau and other West African nations. A fisheries assessment for the Guinea-Bissau government showed sardinella stocks have been decimated, according to the OCCRP report.
