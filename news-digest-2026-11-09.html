Agoa: Why Trump wan remove Rwanda and South Africa from US trade deal

BBC · 3 Nov 2026

The future of what was billed as a game-changing trade agreement between Africa and the US is up for discussion in the South African city of Johannesburg over the next few days, with calls for it to be turned into a much longer-term pact, despite some criticism of the deal.

The African Growth and Opportunity Act (Agoa) was signed into law by former United States President Bill Clinton in May 2000, in a bid to improve trade and investment ties with sub-Saharan Africa on the basis that the best way to raise living standards on the continent and create badly needed jobs, was through trade, not aid.

It allows eligible African countries to export some of their produce to the US without paying taxes, meaning they are cheaper for US consumers to buy, and so they should buy more.

Employment law 5 in 5: October 2026

DLA Piper GENIE · 4 Nov 2026

October brings another significant stage of UK employment reform. Changes under the Employment Rights Act include longer time limits for employment tribunal claims, with the general limit increasing from three to six months from 1 October 2026, alongside further reforms relating to trade unions and harassment. Employers also have more change on the horizon: the government is progressing its plans on dismissal and re-engagement and enhanced protection for pregnant women and new mothers , while a major consultation is considering employment status reform and protections for the self-employed , with responses due by 8 January 2027. A separate government review is examining potential whistleblowing reform , including mandatory workplace policies and possible financial rewards.

Italy has adopted a legislative decree to align its framework with the EU AI Act : employers may not rely solely on AI systems for key decisions such as hiring, dismissals, disciplinary action and performance evaluations and workers must receive clear information about AI systems used. Implementation of the Gender Pay Transparency Directive continues, with the latest Dutch bill making hirers, rather than staffing agencies or other lenders, responsible for reporting on the pay of agency workers. Family protections are expanding too: from 1 October 2026, Finnish employers must automatically give written reasons when a protected employee’s fixed-term employment ends or is not renewed , while Spain has introduced new rights for employees with care responsibilities and new protected sick leave categories for certain menopause-related conditions .

Employers in China that engage workers beyond retirement age should review their arrangements following the Interim Provisions on the Protection of the Basic Rights and Interests of Over-Age Workers , which took effect on 1 July 2026. Although over-age workers are not employees under PRC labour law, they now have core statutory protections that cannot be contracted out of. Overtime limits and overtime pay rates under the PRC Labour Law now apply, and employers must enrol over-age workers in work-related injury insurance and pay the premiums. Employers should review rosters, pay and agreements, and put a formal retirement transition process in place.

North Africa has world's slowest Internet

Connecting Africa · 11 Oct 2024

North Africa has the world's slowest broadband as a collective region followed by sub-Saharan Africa which is the second slowest in the world.

That's according to Cable.co.uk's Worldwide Broadband Speed League 2021 data which shows Africa is seriously lagging other regions. The data comes from over 1.1 billion speed tests taken in the 12 months up to June 30, 2021, and spanning 224 countries.

Northern Africa recorded the lowest overall Internet speeds with a regional average of 5.68 Mbit/s. The fastest region was Western Europe with an average speed of 90.56 Mbit/s. All of North Africa's six qualifying countries were in the bottom half of the table.
