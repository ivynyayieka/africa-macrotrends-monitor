PP106: Democracy: Faltering demand in Africa?

Afrobarometer · 10 Sep 2026

Democracy in Africa has faced considerable challenges over the past decade. According  to the Lexical Index of Electoral Democracy (Skaaning, 2021), while most of the democracies  surveyed by Afrobarometer a decade ago have maintained democratic rule, several have  experienced democratic backsliding in the years since to “multiparty autocracies” (Benin,  Nigeria, Côte d’Ivoire, Mozambique, Sierra Leone, and Tanzania) or military regimes (Burkina  Faso, Madagascar, and Mali). Only one country has improved its position, as Zambia  returned to “electoral democracy” status in 2021 (Herre, Rodés-Guirao, & Ortiz-Ospina,  2026a; France24, 2026). As measured by V-Dem’s (2026) indices of liberal democracy, many  other countries have experienced declines in the quality of electoral contestation and political freedoms over the same period (Herre, Rodés Guirao, & Ortiz-Ospina, 2026b).

These developments raise the question: How hungry are ordinary Africans  for democracy?

Since its inception, Afrobarometer’s surveys have contained sets of questions that measure the extent to which people want to live in a democratically governed society (or what we call “demand for democracy”) and the  degree to which they believe they are living in a democracy (what we have termed the  perceived “supply of democracy”).

Avante! Festival at 50: Portuguese Communists celebrate five decades of democracy and struggle

People's World · 8 Sep 2026

SEIXAL, Portugal—Hundreds of thousands of Communists, workers, youth, and their allies joined the 50th anniversary of the Avante! Festival (Festa do Avante!) from Sept. 4 to 6 in Seixal, Portugal, a suburb just outside the capital, Lisbon.

The event is named for Avante! , or Forward! in English, the newspaper of the Portuguese Communist Party, which organizes the massive gathering.

Held annually, the festival celebrates Portugal’s struggle for democracy after the 1974 Carnation Revolution, a bloodless coup orchestrated by left- and Communist-aligned sections of the Portuguese military. It brought an end to the fascist Estado Novo regime, which ruled the country through terrorism and political repression for over 40 years.
